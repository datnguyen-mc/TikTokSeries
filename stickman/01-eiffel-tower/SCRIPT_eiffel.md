# The Man Who Sold the Eiffel Tower — script

In 1925, a man sold the Eiffel Tower. There was just one problem. He didn't own it.

Back then, the tower was rusting, and keeping it painted cost a fortune. Con man Victor Lustig read a newspaper story about it, and smiled.

He printed fake government letterhead, and named himself a deputy director at the Ministry of Posts and Telegraphs.

Then he booked a room at one of the fanciest hotels in Paris, and invited the city's top scrap metal dealers to a secret meeting.

The government, he said, was quietly selling the tower for scrap. Seven thousand tons of iron. And it had to stay secret, to avoid a public outcry.

One dealer, André Poisson, wanted it badly. So Lustig sighed that government officials were poorly paid, and big decisions were hard to make alone.

Poisson got the hint. In total, Lustig walked away with about seventy thousand dollars, and left Paris within an hour.

And Poisson? He was so embarrassed, he never went to the police.

So Lustig came back, and tried to sell the Eiffel Tower again.

This time, he suspected a dealer had tipped off the police. He fled to America, became a counterfeiter, and ended up in Alcatraz.

Source: Smithsonian Magazine, "The Smoothest Con Man That Ever Lived".
