## Description
In the year 79, Mount Vesuvius woke up after centuries of sleep. In about 32 hours, Pompeii, Herculaneum, Stabiae and Oplontis vanished under ash and stone. In 12 chapters: a busy port town famous for its fish sauce, 17 years of warning earthquakes, the "pine-tree" cloud seen by 17-year-old Pliny, 17 hours of falling pumice, Admiral Pliny the Elder's rescue voyage, the last night at Herculaneum, the last morning, 1,700 years of oblivion, Fiorelli's plaster casts, the mystery of the eruption date and the DNA surprises, the charred library being read by AI – and a volcano that is still awake.

⚠️ This video uses stick figures and AI-generated illustrations to retell historical events; real people appear only as labelled stick figures. It does not depict human remains. The eruption date (24 August or late October, AD 79) is still debated.

## Notes (add to description)
- Estimates of Pompeii's population vary widely (from about 10,000 to over 20,000).
- The 32-hour duration and "just after 7 a.m. on day two" follow Scarpati et al. (2025); earlier studies estimated about 18–19 hours.
- Pyroclastic flow temperatures varied by place and pulse; the film says "hundreds of degrees".
- Aircraft destroyed in 1944: sources range from 78 to 88.
- Vesuvius red zone: Italian Civil Protection uses about 700,000 residents.
- Campi Flegrei M4.7 earthquake on 31 July 2026 (INGV).
