## Description
At midnight tonight, the Moon simply disappears. No explosion, no falling rocks. One second it's there, the next second it isn't.

Would the oceans flood the coasts? Would Earth fly out of its orbit? From the first pitch-black night to millions of years later, here is what would really change: the tides, the coral reefs, the lions of the savanna, baby sea turtles, eclipses, our calendars, the length of a day, and even the tilt that gives us seasons.

👉 Without the Moon, what would you miss most: moonlit nights, the tides, or eclipses? Tell me in the comments!

⚠️ This is a science thought experiment told with animated illustrations. Long-term effects are based on scientific models and estimates, some of which are still debated.

## Notes on content
- The scenario assumes the Moon simply vanishes, with no explosion and no debris.
- The 1993 study (Laskar et al.) suggested a moonless Earth's tilt could swing between about 0° and 85°; later simulations found slower, smaller swings.
- Estimates of a day's length if the Moon had never formed vary widely; the 6–12 hour range is presented as uncertain.
