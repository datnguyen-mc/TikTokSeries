## Description
On December 4th, 1872, sailors in the middle of the Atlantic found a ship drifting with torn sails and no one at the wheel. The cargo was still there. There was food for six months. But all ten people aboard – including the captain's wife and two-year-old daughter – had vanished.

From an unlucky ship and a suspicious investigation in Gibraltar to a famous short story that invented the "warm tea", nine mysterious empty barrels and a possibly faulty clock – here is the true story of the Mary Celeste.

## Notes on content
- Found on December 4, 1872 between the Azores and Portugal; last log entry November 25, 1872 near Santa Maria (Azores).
- The salvage award was £1,700, about one fifth of the value of ship and cargo.
- The half-eaten meals and warm tea come from fiction (Conan Doyle, 1884), not from the original testimony.
- Fume and faulty-chronometer explanations are modern hypotheses; the fate of the ten people is unknown.
