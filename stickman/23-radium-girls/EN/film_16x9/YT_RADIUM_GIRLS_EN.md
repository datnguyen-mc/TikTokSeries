## Description
In the 1920s, hundreds of young American women had what looked like a dream job: painting watch dials with a paint that glowed in the dark. Their bosses told them it was perfectly safe. It was not – and some of the people in charge knew.

This is the true story of the Radium Girls: the "lip, dip, paint" technique, the toothache that started it all, a report that was quietly changed, and the women who took a powerful company to court while they were dying – and changed workplace safety forever.

## Notes on content
- The New Jersey case was settled out of court in June 1928; Catherine Donohue won her Illinois case in April 1938.
- The Harvard (Drinker) report linked the illnesses to radium; a version given to New Jersey officials claimed the workers were in perfect condition.
- Modern glow-in-the-dark watch markings use far safer materials.
