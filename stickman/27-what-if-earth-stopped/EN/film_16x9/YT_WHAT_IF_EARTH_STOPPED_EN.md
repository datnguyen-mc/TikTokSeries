## Description
Right now you are spinning at hundreds of miles per hour – and you don't feel a thing. But what if, one second from now, the Earth just stopped?

In this animated thought experiment: winds four times stronger than the fastest gust ever measured, giant waves racing inland, a single day that lasts a whole year, oceans flowing to the poles, a planet-wide supercontinent – and the surprising twist that the Earth really is slowing down.

👉 Which what-if should I explore next? Tell me in the comments!

⚠️ This is a thought experiment. Nothing in our solar system could stop the Earth all at once.

## Notes on content
- Speeds follow the usual figures: about 1,000 mph at the equator, roughly 600–800 mph at the latitude of New York or Paris.
- The fastest wind gust ever measured was about 253 mph (Barrow Island, 1996).
- Equatorial bulge about 21 km; tidal braking lengthens the day by roughly 1.7–2.3 milliseconds per century.
- The links between Earth's spin and its magnetic field are presented as a scientific idea, not a certainty.
