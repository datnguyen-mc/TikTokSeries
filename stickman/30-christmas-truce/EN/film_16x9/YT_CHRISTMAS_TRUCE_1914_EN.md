## Description
Christmas Eve, 1914. Along the front lines in Belgium, British and German soldiers face each other across frozen mud. Then candles appear on small Christmas trees, a German carol drifts across No Man's Land – and the shooting stops.

The next morning, unarmed soldiers climbed out of their trenches, shook hands, swapped gifts, showed each other family photos – and in some places kicked a football around.

## Notes on content
- The truce was unofficial and varied from place to place; around 100,000 British and German troops are thought to have taken part.
- Pope Benedict XV had called for a Christmas truce; the warring governments did not agree to an official one.
- Football is recorded in several letters and accounts, mostly informal kickabouts.
- Uniforms are simplified and shown without insignia; no combat or injuries are shown.
